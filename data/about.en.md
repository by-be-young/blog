# About Me

You can call me Be Young.

This name originally came from a pen name I picked in elementary school and I've continued to use it since. Over the years it has gained deeper meaning for me; I prefer to leave part of that meaning unspoken.

## Purpose of This Blog

Most blogs I see focus on teaching and guiding study. Many seniors and classmates are talented and accomplished.

I don't claim outstanding talent or achievements, and I can't offer deep insights. This blog is mainly a record of my own study notes and reflections.

Besides study, I occasionally share personal notes and impressions. I don't expect many readers or that anyone will deeply appreciate them—I treat this blog as a personal circle of posts or an electronic diary, a time capsule that preserves small memories.

## Personality

INFP.

This personality has many flaws, I know.

As an INFP, it's hard for me to make friends. I don't like to reach out to strangers, and I fear others seeing my weaknesses, so I tend to withdraw.

Ironically, once I make a friend I want to share everything with them, which can sometimes drive them away.

My mind is filled with a loop of inner conflict: wanting to confide but fearing annoyance, so I often feel lost and isolated.

## Interests

### Games

Until graduating high school I rarely played games. Not because I loved studying so much, but because fragmented time made it hard to keep up with most games.

During the summer after the college entrance exam I downloaded Arknights on a whim. It requires no complex operations or intense competition, and doesn't force interaction with other players. I can enjoy strategic thinking on my own without worrying about others. That was exactly the kind of game I wanted.

Now I'm more invested in Arknights. Its depth and strategy go far beyond casual play, and learning the game feels endless.

### Languages

Languages fascinate me because they enable cross-domain thinking.

Chinese is profound and concise, connecting ancient and modern sensibilities with tonal beauty and parallel structure.

Learning other languages lets you glimpse other cultures. Studying different grammars and logic rewires the mind and offers new perspectives on the same things.

Comparing languages reveals both shared insights and differences in thought and worldview.

That's why this blog supports three language choices and reflects cultural differences in places like dates.

### Music

J-POP changed my life.

Music isn't merely entertainment; it should convey inner feelings and resonate with listeners.

Japan is a center of pop music in Asia. While some places mass-produce shallow products, Japanese pop often pours deep care into each song. An artist might release only a few tracks a year, but each one receives great attention and craft.

I admire that approach. You don't need to listen to thousands of songs—each song can be played a thousand times without getting tired.

I also write songs in my spare time. Although I mostly follow simple formulas, adding my own ideas excites me. I turn feelings into melodies and notes—it's the romantic part of being a creator.

## Final Note

I know life will hold many regrets and I may never reach others' heights, but I can be myself. This blog is my record of study and life.
